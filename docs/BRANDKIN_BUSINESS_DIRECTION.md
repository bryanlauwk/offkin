# Brandkin — agency-first corporate collectibles

Working direction, 30 September 2026. Assumptions below are proposed tests, not validated demand or supplier quotations.

## Promise

Make your client's brand worth keeping. Turn a company story into a collectible that an agency can pitch, sample, and produce for its client.

The agency is the buyer and account owner; its corporate client approves the concept; employees, customers, partners, or event guests receive it. Keep the agency's identity on client-facing documents. A share link still opens the studio; only the client text brief omits studio branding and internal budget. There is no agency account system or white-label hosted portal yet.

## Name search

Brandkin is the preferred working name: brand + kin suggests affinity, belonging, and a family of collectible objects. It accommodates bricks, miniatures, and tactile gifts. Keeptale is an alternative with more emphasis on keepsakes and storytelling. Initial exact-name web searches found no obvious matching business for either; that is not a finding of domain, handle, company-name, or trademark availability.

Do not advance Storymade or Taleform: active adjacent storytelling/design businesses were found at https://www.storymadecreative.com/ and https://www.taleform.co/ . Mementry also has existing use at https://mementry.fashionstore.jp/about . Naming is provisional until a separate availability check in the intended markets.

## Four editions, three physical formats

| Edition | Customer promise | Fictional Rimba Coffee example | Good occasion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Icon | Keep the symbol | Nonfunctional signature cup miniature | Event gift or recognizable brand keepsake |
| Hero | Build the story | Brick stall with an original barista character | Anniversary, employee recognition, founder story |
| Inside | Discover how it works | Hand-operated roasting model; it does not roast coffee | Product launch or customer education |
| Everyday | Make it a daily ritual | Espresso-shaped clicker with phone stand | Onboarding or client appreciation |

Formats are independent of the narrative: brick build, miniature, clicker. Icon excludes clickers because it is the nonfunctional edition. A factual founder or milestone story must be supplied or confirmed. Do not invent history from a company name.

Other agency pitches: logistics — miniature delivery van, driver hero scene, parcel-sorting model, van-shaped desk organizer; solar — sun sculpture, first-rooftop build, panel-to-home explanation, solar-roof phone stand with no charging claim.

## Opportunity and competition

Custom corporate brick gifts already exist. Iconic Bricks offers branded models, instructions, packaging and corporate applications: https://www.iconicbricks.com/custom-kits/ . BusinessMocs offers design, production and packaging: https://businessmocs.com/ . Bricktastics offers branded brick keyrings: https://bricktastics.com/products/branded-logo-keyrings-for-corporate-events-square-format . These vendor pages establish existing supply, not independent proof of demand, retention, or margins.

The proposed differentiation is an agency workflow: quickly develop a story-led pitch, compare narrative directions, keep client communication under the agency's name, and use reusable physical designs to make sampling and repeat production easier. AI renders alone are easy to copy. The longer-term advantage would be validated mechanisms, brick designs, reliable suppliers, quality control, delivery performance and repeat agency relationships.

## First commercial offer

Start as a managed design-and-production studio with software supporting the pitch. Sell a paid concept-and-sample package plus a production quote. Begin with Icon miniatures and simple Everyday clickers using repeatable bases. Offer Hero brick builds with a specialist design partner. Price Inside models individually because engineering effort can vary substantially.

Suggested pilot scope: one client brand, two concept directions, one revision round, one physical sample and a separately quoted first batch. These are proposed commercial terms; minimum quantities and lead times need supplier confirmation.

Potential revenue: creative/design fee; sampling and engineering fee; margin on production and packaging; repeat editions and reorders. Later, agencies with repeat usage may justify retainers. Do not begin by assuming a software subscription is the main revenue source.

Illustrative unit economics only: 100 units at RM80 = RM8,000 product revenue. If landed manufacturing, packing, inspection and defect allowance total RM45 per unit, product contribution is RM3,500 before design labour, sales, overhead, tax and any omitted delivery costs. A separate design fee must cover actual creative hours and revisions. None of these amounts is a supplier quote or market benchmark.

## Four-week validation experiment

1. Interview 8–10 agencies about recent corporate gift projects, client approval delays, budgets, quantities, deadlines and failure points. Ask for real past briefs.
2. Choose three briefs and produce visual concepts plus supplier-backed estimates. Make one physical Icon and one Everyday sample.
3. Seek three paid pilots with deposits covering committed supplier costs. Do not count compliments or free renders as purchase validation.
4. Track pitch-to-paid conversion, design hours, sample revisions, defect/rework cost, contribution after labour, on-time delivery and whether agencies bring a second brief. Continue only if customers pay and delivery economics hold up.

A useful starting hypothesis is agencies serving food, hospitality and lifestyle clients, where recognizable objects and rituals are easier to express. Industrial and technology agencies may pay more for Inside models, but those introduce more engineering work. Validate through the interviews rather than launching every vertical at once.

## Customer interface

Company name/website identifier + supplied facts → story edition → object format → one concept → agency/project details → client-facing or internal brief → design review → quotation → sample approval → production.

The current implementation identifies brands from model knowledge and provided context; it does not crawl websites or verify brand facts. A future research step should show sources, suggest a brand profile and ask the agency to confirm it before generation.

Current scope: agency-oriented creation UI, four editions, three formats, one concept per request, generation metadata and cache separation, client/internal text briefs, and preserved curated clicker links. No fabricated prices, submitted proposals, customer accounts, order taking, email delivery, payment processing or verified manufacturing output.
